# The Cognition Project Podcast

*Shared by Imy*

In case you needed another podcast...

Cognitive science is pervasive in ALife, with many of the field's influential figures and ideas coming from this field. So I found it really cool to see this new podcast that traces the history of cognitive science through oral history interviews with prominent figures in the field. The creator, Tom Griffiths of Princeton University, has developed it in a way that allows it to be adapted or mapped onto undergraduate cognitive science courses, which could be really useful for incoming (or current) students, as well as anybody who is putting such a course together.

From the creator himself:

*Over the last decade I have been doing oral history interviews with cognitive scientists and am releasing them in a podcast called the Cognition Project. Currently we have interviews posted with Jerome Bruner, Molly Potter, Susan Carey, Lila Gleitman, Noam Chomsky, Jean Berko Gleason, Tom Bever, Dan Slobin, Richard Atkinson, Gordon Bower, Roger Shepard, Rich Shiffrin, Elizabeth Loftus, Ewart Thomas, Phil Johnson-Laird, Donald Norman, Eleanor Rosch, and Douglas Hofstadter. The interviews so far have focused on the cognitive revolution, mathematical psychology, and the first cognitive science conferences, but we’re moving into a section on different approaches to AI and the origins of neural networks. The interviews are designed to be accessible to undergraduates so they can be assigned in classes where you cover the work of these researchers.*
