## 'Infinite Search Space' Contest (January 31, 2027)

(Shared by Neil Brewer, Infinite Search Space)

**Paper**: Christopher G. Langton, ["Studying artificial life with cellular
automata,"](https://doi.org/10.1016/0167-2789(86)90237-X) Physica D 22 (1986),
120–149.

**Summary**: Langton's paper is a manifesto for studying life-like
behavior from the bottom up, in simple local rules rather than in
top-down models. In passing it introduces a "virtual ant" on a grid of
two-colored cells: on a white cell the ant turns right, on a black
cell it turns left; either way it flips the cell's color and steps
forward. That is the entire rule. From a blank grid the ant wanders
chaotically for about 10,000 steps, then suddenly settles into
building a straight diagonal "highway," repeating a 104-step cycle
forever.

**Why I love it**: Forty years on, the paper's throwaway example is still
a reminder of how little we can predict from a rule we fully
understand. It's proven that the ant's path is always unbounded, but
the "highway conjecture" (that it always ends up building a highway,
from any finite starting pattern) is still open. Generalize to more
colors and the zoo gets stranger: symmetric growth, spirals, chaotic
blobs that never settle.

That gap between a rule you can write on a napkin and behavior nobody
can predict is exactly what makes a good optimization puzzle, so I've
built a contest on it. 'Infinite Search Space' has run open-ended
programming contests since 2011 (with a long break in between), and
our new one, Ant Escape, uses a four-color generalization in which
each cell's color says how far the ant turns: straight on, right,
about-face or left. You design the whole grid, on every size from
10×10 to 34×34, and choose the row where the ant walks in from the
left. Your score is how many steps it takes the ant to find its way
out. The ant can never loop forever, so every grid has an answer;
finding a long one is the hard part.

It runs until January 31, 2027. It's free and has no ads, and there
are two leaderboards: one for people who work it out themselves and
one for people who use AI. Entries are checked by an open scorer, and
every solution is published when the contest closes.

[Contest Website](https://infinitesearchspace.com/current)

And to watch the classic ant find its highway, or to play with other
artificial life, there are [free toys in our
Lab](https://infinitesearchspace.com/lab?topic=Artificial%20Life)
